📚 Built on the research

80/20 polarized training (Dr. Stephen Seiler): ~80% of endurance work stays easy and conversational, ~20% goes truly hard. The gray zone in between is where progress dies — so your easy days are deliberately easy and your interval/tempo days are the only hard ones.

Fat loss that keeps muscle: research on resistance-trained people supports losing ~0.5–1% of bodyweight per week with high protein (~0.7–1 g per lb — 160–180 g for you, split across 3–5 meals) plus lifting. That combo is what protects lean mass while the fat leaves — which is exactly what makes 170 look like a six-pack.

Progressive overload + deloads: volume climbs for 3 weeks, then every 4th week backs off so your body absorbs the work. Strength uses double progression — own the top of the rep range, then add weight.

Isaac's plan — NSCA youth guidelines: kids ready for sports (around age 7–8) are ready for supervised, form-first, bodyweight strength training, and it benefits bone and long-term athleticism. His engine: movement variety, low reps, fun over max effort, never maxing out.
